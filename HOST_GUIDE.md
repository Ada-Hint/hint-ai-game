# A Hint of AI — your 18-minute run sheet

## Before the session

**Three business days before:** sign in to Manage → Edit survey. Adjust the wording, choices, and multi-answer settings; preview and publish. Copy the survey link and invitation from Setup & links into your Teams invitation. Add the response deadline and session time yourself.

**The afternoon before:** close responses. Review frequency, tool usage, and confidence to understand the group’s starting point. Group any Other entries under meaningful categories, counting each person once per category. Only approve written excerpts you are comfortable reading to the whole room. If you change published questions after collecting answers, the new version starts fresh—use the editor before sending the survey wherever possible.

**Before the call:** open rehearsal from Setup & links. Practice with a host tab, a presentation tab, and a player on a second browser/phone. Use its separate DEMO code. When you are ready to run the real event, select **Run game → Start game**. This closes the survey, saves its results, and shows the first card. The timer waits for you to open voting. Copy the player link into your meeting notes.

**Five minutes before:** open the hosted app so it is awake. Sign in to Manage in one tab. Open the presentation link in a second tab. Share **only the presentation tab/window** in Teams, then put the player link and event code in Teams chat. Attendees can vote beside Teams on a laptop or scan the QR code with a phone.

## Turn written responses into game answers

Use the **open-ended workshop survey** draft for the proposed 13 questions. The first question uses work-area tick boxes; AI comfort uses a 1–10 scale; tools, current usage, and learning topics allow multiple selections and Other text. The eight open-text prompts ask for one answer each and are optional by default. Adjust Required in Edit survey if desired.

After collecting responses, open **Survey results** and expand a text question. Group similar answers with **Count this response under → ＋ Create a category**, then reuse the category for related answers. For example, “weekly sales report” and “pulling sales numbers” could both go under “Reporting.” **Exclude from game counts** explicitly removes an answer from the game tally. The app shows anything still ungrouped.

Enter your game question and click **＋ Guess the room** or **＋ Rank the room** under those results. Adjust the card and its place in **Game content**. Review or remove older survey-linked cards if you replaced their source questions. You can build rounds after the survey closes; you do not need to decide all the categories or game questions before collecting answers.

Each person counts once per category. Text-question percentages use respondents with grouped answers. Before starting a linked text round, review or exclude every entry and have at least two categories with answers. At least five grouped respondents are needed for scored survey rounds; Guess the room can instead become an unscored poll when fewer people answered. Original wording stays private unless you approve an excerpt for the walkthrough.

## Minute-by-minute

| Time | What to do | What to say |
|---|---|---|
| 0–2 | Let people join. Click **Start game**, then **Open voting · 30 seconds**. | “Choose a nickname and save your rejoin code. This is individual, with no speed bonus. Your survey answers aren’t connected to your game score. Any starting point is welcome.” |
| 2–5 | Run both Guess the room questions. Close, reveal, then reveal additional ranked answers. | “Which task did the most colleagues select?” Then: “Which hesitation came up most?” Explain that people could choose several categories, so percentages can exceed 100% in total. |
| 5–9 | Run the creative/brand and lifecycle questions. Pause at the halfway leaderboard. | “A good brief gives a task, audience, source, tone, and format.” Then: “The first answer is a draft. Specific feedback beats ‘try harder.’” |
| 9–13 | Run analytics and creator-brief questions. | “A confident explanation still needs evidence.” Then: “Check product claims against approved facts. Another AI agreeing isn’t verification.” |
| 13–16 | Advance through the four walkthrough steps; run the follow-up poll. | “Open either chat with your appropriate work account. Try a small task using fictional or approved information. Read the result as an editor and ask for a specific change.” |
| 16–18 | Run the commitment poll, then show the final leaderboard and copyable prompts. | “Pick one task you’ll try this week. You don’t have to automate everything. One useful first draft is a good start.” |

Allow up to two extra minutes for discussion. Keep each scored question to roughly 20 seconds to read, 30 seconds to vote, and 30–45 seconds to explain. Scores come from correctness, not speed. Tied answers/ranks are allowed; no tie-breaker is needed.

## The controls

- **Start game:** saves the survey results and shows the first card. The status changes to **GAME RUNNING**. No timer starts yet.
- **Open voting · 30 seconds:** starts the timer. Watch the countdown and player answer count. **＋ 15 seconds** extends it; after voting closes, **Reopen · 15 seconds** reopens it before the reveal.
- **Close voting:** locks votes. **Reveal results:** shows results and awards points once.
- **Reveal next / all:** uncovers the ranked survey board. **Next card →:** advances the room together.
- **Next walkthrough step:** reveals each prepared demonstration stage. These are labeled examples, not a live model run.
- **Finish game:** completes the session and leaves the final scores available.
- **Reset game / unlock editing → Reset game:** clears players, votes, and scores after confirmation, but preserves surveys. Use a separate EVENT_ID for a wholly new event/population.

## Add a ranking round

In **Edit survey**, choose **Ranking (drag to order)** and list the tasks people should rank. Publish and collect responses. Results show average position; lower means higher ranked.

In **Game content → ＋ Add a ranking round**, choose **Survey results**, select the question, and set how many top answers to include. You can also use an existing choice question (ranked by response count), or choose **Host-set order** and enter the correct positions yourself. Survey-based ranking rounds need at least five responses to that question; use a host-set order if there is not enough data. Save and move the card to the desired place before starting the game.

Tell players: “Drag these tasks into the order you think the room ranked them, then press Save ranking. You get 20 points for each item in the correct position.” The up/down buttons work without dragging. Allow extra time with **+15 seconds** if needed. **Reveal results** shows the complete order and awards points; survey ties accept either order within the tied positions, and ties at the top-answer cutoff are all included. Standard quiz questions still award 100 points.

The primary action always appears at the top of **Run game**, beside the game status. The audience preview below shows what the room sees. Opening the presentation board only opens a viewing tab; it does not start or advance the game.

## The answer key

1. **Creative/brand — C.** Supply the brief, audience, tone, length, and approved facts.
2. **DTC/lifecycle — B.** Explain what missed, supply a tone example, and ask for a shorter revision with one CTA.
3. **Analytics/growth — C.** Check calculations and separate observations from possible explanations.
4. **Social/partnerships — A.** Remove unsupported benefits and verify remaining claims against the brief.

Survey answers come from the frozen response counts; any answer tied for the highest count wins. Warm-up, follow-up, and commitment polls have no wrong answer and award no points. If fewer than five people answered a survey-mapped question, that opening becomes an unscored live poll automatically.

## Walkthrough backup

Fictional brief: “Invite the marketing team to a 15-minute creative brainstorm Thursday at noon. Ask each person to bring one idea. Tone: playful and encouraging. No product claims or offers.”

Prompt: “Using this brief, write an internal invitation under 50 words with one clear CTA. Use only the supplied facts; flag missing information.”

Prepared draft: “A little idea time? Join the marketing team for a 15-minute creative brainstorm on Thursday at noon. Bring one idea—rough edges welcome. Let’s see what we can make together.” The meeting link and time zone still need to be supplied.

Follow-up: “Keep the welcoming tone. Make it under 35 words and make ‘bring one idea’ the clear CTA. Give me two alternatives. Keep the missing-information note separate.”

Takeaway: these same habits apply to a creative brief, caption, lifecycle email, or performance recap. Use your organization’s approved accounts and information-sharing rules; account capabilities vary, so this example relies on plain chat only.

## If something goes wrong

- **Someone joins late:** they enter the current question, with no penalty for missed questions.
- **Someone refreshes:** they use the private rejoin code they saved. They should not create a second nickname.
- **Few survey responses:** the opening live polls still work; don’t present sample data as real results.
- **Voting is unreliable but the host still has access:** choose Continue in Teams chat. Ask people to type A/B/C on your count. Previous points stay; later questions become unscored. Continue the same reveal/advance sequence.
- **The app or database is entirely unavailable:** keep the conversation going from this guide. Read the four challenges and walkthrough, collect answers in Teams chat, and skip the final leaderboard until service returns.

## Starter to paste into Teams afterward

```text
Help me create [deliverable] for [audience].
Use the sample information below.
Keep the tone [tone] and return [format/length].
Use only the supplied facts and flag missing information.

Sample information: [paste here]

Follow-up: Keep [what worked], change [what didn’t], and give me two alternatives.
```
